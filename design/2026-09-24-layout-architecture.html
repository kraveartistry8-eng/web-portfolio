01

Column grid

Twelve columns, because it divides by two, three and four. The three-up rows, the half splits and the asymmetric work section all land on it without fractional columns.

1080px page max28px gutter1024px content 24px column gap63.3px column

Running text never uses all twelve. Body copy is capped at 68ch, which lands around column 7. The empty columns to the right are not wasted space, they are the mechanism the research actually supports for a premium read.

03

Where the eight sections sit

Solid marks the primary block, mid-tone the secondary, pale the columns left empty. The work section is deliberately the only asymmetric row on the page.

01 · Hero

cols 1–8 · 320px of air below

Headline capped at 15ch so it breaks to four lines at display size. Four columns stay empty on the right, permanently. One idea, one action, nothing else above the fold.

02 · Three services

3 × 4 cols, equal

Equal weight is correct here and only here: the three services genuinely rank the same, and the visitor is choosing which describes them.

03 · The work

cols 1–7 lead, 8–12 stacked

Three equal tiles would say all three pieces matter the same. They do not. The lead piece takes seven columns at full bleed height, the other two stack in the remaining five. This is the only asymmetric row and it is the section that has to carry the most weight.

04 · Made, not bought

cols 1–7

Pure running text at full measure. No card, no panel. The argument is the only thing on the row.

05 · How it works

cols 1–2 numbers, 3–9 content

Step numbers live in the left margin rather than inline, so the steps read as a sequence at a glance without numbered pills doing the work.

06 · What I don't build

cols 1–6

Narrowest block on the page. A short list at half width reads as decided rather than defensive.

07 · Pricing

3 × 4 cols, matching services

Deliberately the same geometry as section 02, so price maps onto service without a label explaining the correspondence.

08 · Start a project

cols 1–6

Form fields at half width. A field stretched to 1024px looks like work; at six columns it looks like three questions.
