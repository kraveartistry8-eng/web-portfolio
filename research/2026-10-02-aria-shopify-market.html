Tier 2 custom theme builds · Tier 3 Plus and headless

The market is splitting, not shrinking.

Basic Shopify work has had the floor fall out from under it. Specialized work is busier and better paid than it has ever been. Skipping tier one is the correct read, and the data on it is unusually clear.

Tier 2 · custom theme $10k–$50k Design-led studios charge $250 to $500 an hour and run $15k to $150k a project. Your $30k target sits comfortably inside this.
Tier 3 · Hydrogen $50k–$260k plus $3.9k–$13k a month maintenance Ten to twenty-four-plus weeks. React-based, which means your existing stack transfers directly.
The catch ~75% Share of merchants asking for headless who get steered back to standard Shopify Plus by the agencies that build headless.

Your stack already transfers

Hydrogen is React-based. The Astro, GSAP and Three.js work you have been doing maps directly onto this tier and almost not at all onto tier one. The craft you spent two weeks proving on the cube is the craft tier three actually pays for. Brands running Hydrogen at scale include Allbirds, SKIMS, Gymshark, Good American and Denim Tears.

And the buyer is the audience you already picked

Hydrogen's buyer profile is large DTC brands at $5m to $30m-plus revenue needing custom UX beyond what Liquid allows. That is near word for word the audience you defined for the bespoke positioning. You may not be changing markets at all. You may be changing the platform you serve them on.

But headless requires something your audience does not have

The buyer has to run a front-end engineering function, own a release pipeline and absorb ongoing maintenance complexity. A $5m DTC brand with a founder and a small marketing team, which is exactly the buyer you defined, usually cannot. There is real tension between your stated audience and tier three's actual entry requirement, and it is the thing most likely to make a confident plan quietly fail.

The remote fallback is thinner than the listing count

There are over a thousand remote Shopify listings, which sounds abundant. But only 13% of Shopify developer roles are remote at all. 81% are physical and 6% hybrid. If remote employment is the safety net under the plan, it is a narrower net than it appears.

04

The devil's advocate

Evidence that cuts against the thesis, found deliberately.

  1. The bifurcation thesis has a self-interested primary source

    The sharpest version of "basic work is dying, specialized work is booming" comes from an Upwork Top Rated Plus developer with $300k earned who sells specialized services. He concedes it is personal pipeline observation rather than market data. The direction is corroborated by the rate spreads above, but treat the framing as directional, not measured.

  2. Upwork itself is contracting

    Q2 2026 showed year-over-year declines in gross services volume, revenue and active clients. More than half the businesses paying freelancers on Upwork and Fiverr in 2022 had stopped entirely by 2025. Upwork cut one in four of its own jobs. Any plan that routes through marketplaces is routing through a shrinking channel.

  3. The agencies who build headless talk most clients out of it

    One agency that builds headless storefronts steers roughly 75% of merchants who ask for it back to native Shopify Plus. 78% of Shopify domains already have good time-to-first-byte on Liquid. The consistent recommendation across sources is to stay on Liquid unless headless solves a problem Liquid genuinely blocks. Tier three's addressable market is roughly a quarter the size of the people asking for it.

  4. The number that moved you is the floor

    Your friend's $10,000 deal sits at the bottom of tier two. Nothing in this research says $10k is lucrative for a US-based design-led operator. It says $10k is entry.

  5. One cited figure did not survive checking

    "Software development gigs fell 21% post-ChatGPT" appears only in the self-interested source and could not be traced to a primary. Do not repeat it in a proposal or a pitch.

06

Gaps

Stated rather than papered over.

  • Insufficient data available for the absolute size of the tier 3 market, how many Hydrogen builds happen per year, and what share run through agencies versus direct.
  • Insufficient data available for whether the Shopify Partner Directory generates real inbound for a solo operator with no reviews. Listing is free and needs no portfolio, but nothing found quantifies lead flow at the bottom of the directory.
  • Not verified: the 21% decline in software development gigs. Single self-interested source, no primary.
  • Not researched: your runway, your monthly number, your current income, or how many hours a week you have. Those were asked for and not yet given, and no plan can be evaluated without them.
07

What this means for you

Tier 2 and tier 3 are a sequence, not a choice.

Running at both at once, solo, with zero Shopify case studies, is the capacity trap I would expect to break you. Tier two earns cash and produces the case studies. Tier three, entered through agency subcontracting, buys the credential at a lower hourly rate than you could charge directly. One funds the other. They do not run in parallel.

The strongest single fact in this report is that your existing craft transfers to tier three and not to tier one. That is an argument for where you aim, not for where you start.

The addendum changes one thing about this. Retainers at five to twenty thousand a month mean the goal may not be a bigger project at all. It may be two or three brands on monthly retainers, which is a smaller number to sell and a far steadier business to run alone.

And the demo problem did not go away, it followed you. You still have to build something before anyone pays you, on either tier. The difference is that a Shopify demo has a constrained brief and merchants are everywhere, which may make it a genuinely easier first build than the bespoke demo you have been circling for two weeks.

Addendum · recruiter-side data

Added after the first pass, from Constant Hire's Shopify developer salary study dated 8 April 2026, built from their own candidate interviews rather than job-board scraping. It reaches the same tier conclusion from the hiring side rather than the agency side, and adds one cut neither source had.

Retainers were not in the picture before this

Your target band, $5M to $50M, maps to five to twenty thousand dollars a month, recurring. That is a different business from selling $30k projects one at a time, and a materially better one for a solo operator, because it is the thing that solves the feast and famine problem that kills most of them. It is the single most useful number in this whole report.

They put the headless premium at 50% to 100% over Liquid. Two independent sources, approached from opposite ends of the market, landing on the same split.

A dated opening worth taking seriously

They name 2026 migration work, specifically Checkout Extensibility and Shopify Functions, as a current demand spike. For someone with no Shopify case studies, a wave that has just started is a far better entry than one that broke five years ago. Nobody has ten years of experience in a skill that is two years old.

And a named channel into your own audience

Constant Hire recruits specifically into DTC and CPG brands, placing tech and creative roles alongside marketing. That is a concrete door into exactly the audience you already chose, rather than a job board. Relevant only if the employment fallback matters to you.

Three cautions on this source

It is a recruiter. They are paid on placement, usually a percentage of first-year salary, so they have a direct financial interest in benchmarks reading high. Treat these as the top of a real range rather than the middle.

Their hourly figures are salaries divided into hours, not freelance rates. $36–$46 junior and $48–$67 mid look like they contradict the $78–$133 in section 03. They do not. They are a different measurement. Never quote them at a client.

Two of their statistics do not survive scrutiny. "AI-enhanced roles pay 60% to 145% more" has a range too wide to be a measurement and states no method. "78% of candidates expect base salaries above $100,000" measures what candidates want, not what anyone pays. Do not repeat either.
