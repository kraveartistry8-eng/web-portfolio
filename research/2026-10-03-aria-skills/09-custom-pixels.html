01

What it actually is

checkout.liquid was deprecated in two stages. Information, Shipping and Payment went on 13 August 2024, with automatic upgrades from 6 January 2025 that discarded those customisations. Thank you and Order status followed on 28 August 2025, which also covered script tags and additional scripts. Any analytics loaded through checkout.liquid, which for most stores meant Google Tag Manager, stopped collecting at those dates.

The Web Pixels API replaced it. Pixels run inside a sandbox and subscribe to customer events. There are two kinds. An app pixel runs in a strict sandbox, is registered through the @shopify/web-pixels-extension package with a register() function, and reads its configuration from the Admin API. A custom pixel runs in a lax sandbox, is written directly in the admin's pixel manager, needs no boilerplate, and cannot access the settings property.

Both receive init.data, which contains Cart and Customer objects at page render, and both can reach cookies, localStorage and sessionStorage through the browser API. Events include page and product views and the checkout progression events.

02

Does the claim hold

Substantially yes. Custom Pixels and server-side forwarding are genuinely how you restore measurement after checkout.liquid.

"PII data flow" is the loose part. Shopify restricted that access deliberately, and pixels operate under the customer privacy and consent layer rather than having free run of the page. Customer data is present in init.data, so the claim is not wrong, but describing the job as restoring PII is the wrong thing to say to a merchant. What you restore is reliable event measurement.

03

The devil's advocate

  1. This underwrites the business, not a feature

    Section 03 says the monthly page of numbers is "the document that makes renewal a formality instead of a conversation." That page needs event data from a checkout you no longer have raw access to. If a client's tracking broke in 2024 and was never rebuilt, month one produces either no page or a page you cannot stand behind.

  2. Consent makes the data incomplete by design

    Some visitors will not be tracked, and that is correct behaviour rather than a bug. Any before-and-after comparison you publish has to account for it. Quietly presenting partial data as complete is the kind of number section 06 tells you not to stand behind.

  3. It is also an unusually good door-opener

    Broken checkout analytics is diagnosable from outside, has a date attached, and costs the merchant money they can feel. That is the same shape as the Scripts gap, and better than most design observations, because the merchant already suspects their numbers are wrong.
