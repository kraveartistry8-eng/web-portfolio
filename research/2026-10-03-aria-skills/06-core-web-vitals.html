A real metric carrying an invented number

The thresholds are checkable. The business claim is not.

INP is well defined and easy to verify, which makes it one of the few things you can say to a client and have them confirm. The conversion claim attached to it in the table has no source anywhere.

01

What it actually is

Interaction to Next Paint measures a page's responsiveness across all click, tap and keyboard interactions during a visit, from the moment a user acts to the moment the browser paints the next frame. It covers input delay, event handler processing and rendering.

web.dev sets the thresholds precisely: 200ms or under is good, above 200ms to 500ms needs improvement, above 500ms is poor. INP is the successor metric to First Input Delay, which it replaced because FID measured only the first interaction.

The other two Core Web Vitals are Largest Contentful Paint and Cumulative Layout Shift. Your plan already checks CLS in week 6, where the instruction is to validate zero cumulative layout shift because Plus brands reject on it.

02

Does the claim hold

The metric half is accurate and verifiable on a primary source. The business half is not. web.dev's INP article makes no claim at all about conversion rates or search rankings.

"Poor INP directly hurts conversion rates and search engine rankings" is the exact pattern your roadmap's section 06 exists to catch: a real finding carrying a causal sentence it cannot support. Core Web Vitals are a ranking input and a weak one by Google's own description, and every conversion figure attached to them in circulation comes from a party selling performance work.

03

The devil's advocate

  1. Its value to you is that it is checkable, not that it is persuasive

    A measured INP of 180ms is a fact the client can verify in a minute. A claimed conversion lift is a number they can only take on faith, and the sophisticated ones will not. Your positioning is judgment with measurement attached. This metric is the cleanest example of that you have.

  2. Theme JavaScript is rarely the cause

    A restrained theme will usually pass INP. The thing that breaks it is the merchant's app stack competing for the main thread, which you did not install and may not be able to remove. That is worth knowing before you promise a number on a store you did not build.

  3. Checking CLS but not INP is an odd gap

    Week 6 already validates layout shift. Adding INP to the same check costs nothing, uses the same tooling, and closes the only Core Web Vital your plan measures against a brand's actual rejection criteria.

What to say and what not to say

Say: this build measures 180ms INP and zero layout shift, here is the report, check it yourself. Do not say: poor performance costs you x% of revenue. The first is verifiable and makes you look rigorous. The second is the thing the one buyer worth having will check and find unsupported.
