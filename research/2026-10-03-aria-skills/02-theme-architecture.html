01

What it actually is

Online Store 2.0 introduced sections with JSON templates, letting merchants reorder page content. Theme blocks go further. They live as individual Liquid files in a /blocks directory, each holding markup, a {% schema %} and optional {% stylesheet %}, and they are reusable across different sections rather than being locked to the section that defined them. They also nest inside one another to create hierarchy.

A theme block needs a preset defined before it appears in the theme editor's block picker. That detail is the difference between a block a merchant can find and one only you know exists.

Three themes matter right now. Horizon is the default for new stores and the reference implementation of the block architecture. Skeleton is what shopify theme init clones and what shopify.dev names as the recommended starting point for new theme development. Dawn is the previous default, still supported with bug fixes, no longer receiving new features.

02

Does the claim hold

Yes, with two corrections to the wording. "2.0 Sections + Blocks" undersells what changed, because the reusable, nestable theme block is the newer and more consequential unit. And the update is Horizon, singular.

"Merchant-editable layouts" is the right framing and the most commercially important phrase in the whole table. It is the thing a merchant actually experiences after you leave.

03

The devil's advocate

  1. Editability is a support-cost decision disguised as a design decision

    Your care plan explicitly excludes content entry and support tickets, calling that a $200 maintenance plan and a different supplier. That exclusion only survives if the merchant can change their own content. A beautiful theme they cannot edit converts your retainer into the exact low-value work you refused.

  2. Forking Horizon is the tempting wrong move

    Horizon is the thing merchants run, so copying it looks like the shortcut. It is a large codebase you did not write, and the portfolio piece exists so you can explain every decision in it to an agency principal. Skeleton is minimal and yours. Read Horizon, do not fork it.

  3. Nesting depth is undocumented

    The shopify.dev page demonstrates blocks inside blocks but does not state a maximum. Secondary sources claim eight levels in Horizon. [Low Confidence] on that figure. It matters only if you design a deeply nested system and discover a ceiling late.
