The services section

Four ways to build the cube.

Three faces are services, three are work, and opposite faces pair a service with the piece that proves it. At rest the cube turns slowly enough to read, holding about four seconds on each face. Hover any stage below to stop it.

Fixed

Six faces, alternating. Slow resting rotation. Click a face to open an overlay, never a new page. Service copy repeats in the hero.

Open

What a face is made of. Each direction answers “preview as an icon” differently, so picking one settles it.

A

Direction A

The Solid

An opaque die. Work faces are full-bleed in the demo’s own colour, service faces are white with mono type. The cube visibly alternates hot and cold as it turns.

What makes it interesting

Token rule 4 becomes an object. Colour arrives only from the work, and the alternation is the design system rotating in place. It is also the only direction that reads as a real physical thing, so a kick reads as physics rather than as a screensaver.

Hierarchy

The face pointed at you wins outright, which is what you want when a click has to be unambiguous. No competing focal point.

What it costs

It is the direction most dependent on the demos existing. Until one ships, half the cube is a flat colour swatch with a label on it, and that reads as unfinished rather than as restraint.

Preview means

A full-bleed screenshot, cropped to the face.

B

Direction B

The Frame

Edges only. Six hairline planes in accent, faces near-transparent, each work carried by a small plate floating on its plane. You see through the cube to the faces behind it.

What makes it interesting

It is the page’s one repeated gesture, the 1px rule, turned into an object. Nothing else on the site would have to change for this to belong. It looks considered before a single demo exists.

Hierarchy

Weakest of the four. Every face is partly visible at all times, so nothing dominates, and the graphic-design rule that equal weight is not a design applies directly.

What it costs

Seeing work through other work, reversed, is noise. And a click target you can see through is a click target you can misjudge, which is the single riskiest thing in a feature whose whole point is clicking a face.

Preview means

A small plate or mark, never a screenshot.

C

Direction C

The Net

At rest it is not a cube. It is the cube unfolded flat, all six faces readable at once. Grab it and it folds up, becomes solid, and becomes kickable. Let go and it unfolds again.

What makes it interesting

The fold is the most memorable single gesture available in this feature, and it is one nobody expects. Everything is legible instantly with no waiting for a rotation, and the toy becomes opt-in rather than compulsory.

Hierarchy

Flat and even at rest, which suits six items that genuinely rank the same. The fold supplies the drama instead of the layout.

What it costs

It breaks the resting rotation you asked for, deliberately, and it is by some distance the most build. Six faces folding along five hinges without tearing is the hardest thing on the page.

Preview means

Either. The net can hold screenshots or marks.

D

Direction D

The Plate

Every face is neutral. A drawn mark in line weight, a mono index above, a name below. No screenshots anywhere on the cube. The work’s own colour arrives only when the overlay opens.

What makes it interesting

It ships today. Nothing on this cube waits for a demo, so the section is finished before the work is. Space, type and restraint are also the three levers that actually have evidence behind reading as expensive.

Hierarchy

Even by construction, and the click opens the colour, which makes the overlay feel like a reward rather than a page change.

What it costs

It is quiet. Six line drawings turning slowly may not earn the attention a cube demands, and a set of icons this uniform risks reading as a settings menu rather than as work.

Preview means

A drawn mark, made by you, one per demo.
