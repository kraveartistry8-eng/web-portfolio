Big cube · type attachment · 24 Sep 2026

Upright or attached. Pick where on the line.

Same cube, same speed, four ways of handling the type. Upright type is the one element turning against its own face, and that relative slide is what made it read as a separate plane. These four trade uprightness for attachment in different amounts. Hover to freeze.

B1Printed No correction at all. Type is locked to the face and rolls with it, exactly like ink on a real die. Maximum attachment, and it will be upside down a good share of the time.
B2Quarter turn Type stays parallel to the face’s own edges at all times, but jumps to whichever of the four edge directions is nearest upright. Still printed on the surface, never more than 45° off level.
B3Damped Two thirds of the way to upright. Keeps a third of the face’s roll so the type still moves with the surface rather than against it, while never leaning far enough to be hard to read.
B4Upright, bound Fully upright as before, but each face now carries an inset rule that rolls with the cube, and the type sits a shade into the surface colour. The rule gives the eye a face-locked edge right beside the type, which is the thing that was missing.
