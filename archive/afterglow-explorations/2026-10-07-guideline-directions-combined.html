Three ways the specimen meets the fall

The direction is settled: one continuous fall from gold to deep violet, with every rule proved on a field of the colour being specified. What is not settled is how a specimen sits on a ground that is already moving. These are three answers.

All three spreads show the same slice of the same document, t 0.360 to 0.720, so the comparison is about structure and nothing else. Same ground values, same crossover, same copy. Type is the placeholder stack; faces are a later decision and are not pre-empted here.

The measured problem all three are answering

The fall passes through rose. A rose specimen panel laid on the ground at t 0.5850, where the ground is #AD2E37, measures 1.15:1 against it and all but disappears. Deep violet, which the fall has not reached yet, measures 2.12:1 in the same spot, and gold 3.22:1. The spec's line that specimens “sit on whatever ground the fall has reached” has a failure mode, and it is worst for the stage most likely to need demonstrating.

One thing all three share, and that no direction can settle. At 390px the regions are placed by flow rather than at a stated position, so a printed number tied to a position stops being true. It shows up in iteration 1: the panel is centred at t 0.5850 on a wide screen and wherever the copy leaves room on a narrow one. Making a ratio a function of document position rather than a constant is work the build has to do.

The specimen is an object on the page

A bordered rectangle of the colour being specified, inset into the measure, carrying its own data. Closest to how Frame.io and every foundry specimen handle a swatch.

It is the only one of the three that can show a stage the fall has not reached, so deep violet and gold are as easy to place as rose.

It also discloses its own weakness in print: the 1.15:1 is on the panel, and the hairline exists because of it.

Cost. A border on every specimen is a lot of hairlines, and a rule the document needs in order to work is a rule the brand did not ask for.

The page is the sample, the margin is the caption

No panels at all. Data sits in a void ledger at exactly the height where the fall equals the value it describes, with a leader rule connecting the two.

The 1.15:1 problem cannot occur, because nothing is laid on the ground. Labels run at 11.01:1 on void instead of fighting a moving field.

It is also the only one where the measurement is visibly taken from the document rather than printed next to it.

Cost. It can only specify colours the fall actually passes through at that point. Gold and deep violet need a different spread, and the measure loses about a quarter of its width.

The specimen is a position, not a panel

Full bleed, flush with the fall, placed where the descent passes through the stage. The 1.15:1 becomes 1.00:1 by construction and the invisibility stops being a defect.

The strongest version of the direction's own argument: the document does not illustrate the rule, it is a reading of it. Nothing is quoted.

Also the quietest. Two hairlines and a row of figures is the entire apparatus.

Cost. The most constrained. Each stage can appear at exactly one place in the document, which means the fall's shape dictates the running order of the content, not the other way round.
