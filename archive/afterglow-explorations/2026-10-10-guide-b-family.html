Every sheet carries a photograph.

Four ways of placing the photograph on a spec sheet. Content, order and pages are the same in all four. Only how the image sits on the sheet changes.

No repeats36 photographs, each used once, checked at build. The 24 stage references stay on the stage spreads, and every other slot takes one of 12 frames not used before.
Art direction leadsThe lamp throwing its shadow down the wall is "one light source, and it is falling" as a photograph. A three-frame strip shows warm to cool.
Ends on voidThe ground falls gold to violet across twelve sheets. Digital and print, then voice, are fully black.
A sliver of magentaThe fall band runs gold, after gold, rose, violet, void, and ends on a sliver of #7A1F6B.
B1

Column

The photograph is a tall column beside the title, and the readings take the rest of the sheet.

The calmest of the four. The image and the readings never touch, so neither competes. Art direction runs the lamp half-bleed off the top-left corner.

B2

Edge

The photograph bleeds off the sheet edge at full height, and the side alternates from sheet to sheet.

Turning pages has a rhythm, like a magazine spread. The image is larger than in B1 and runs to the paper's edge. Art direction gives the lamp 58% of the sheet.

B3

Band

The photograph is a letterbox across the top of every sheet, like a film still, with the readings below.

The most cinematic of the four. On art direction the lamp fills two-thirds of the sheet. The cost: portrait frames lose most of themselves to a wide crop, and the band narrows on the type and grid sheets to make room.

B4

Inset

The photograph is the ground of every sheet, and the readings sit over it in void panels.

This answers the "black boxes" problem: the panels become the legible surface over an image instead of boxes on coloured paper. The cost is that the readings cover a lot of each photograph.

Open questions

  • The magenta breaks two of the guide's own rules: the fall says four stops, never added to. And it is not a token. If it stays, it becomes a fifth stop in design-tokens.json and the fall rules get rewritten. It is #7A1F6B, not #FF00BD, which the tokens forbid.
  • Three frames are low resolution for full-bleed print: the cover (1,100 px wide), the lamp (1,100 px) and the wordmark texture (420 px). Higher-resolution originals are needed before the final build.
  • Critics named the stage spreads (sheets 08–11) as the weakest pages: four identical 3×2 grids with no lead frame. That layout was approved with the imagery section, so it is unchanged here, but it is worth revisiting.
